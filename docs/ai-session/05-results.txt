  # Hub weather exposure: summary for the hand-over

  **The question.** Which of the twelve distribution hubs are most exposed to
  weather disruption? The system ranks the hubs, explains the ranking and says
  what the records cannot show, to support this year's choice of hubs for
  resilience investment. It does not say where to invest.

  ## The ranking today

  The score is **expected disruption days a year**: the days a hub spent under
  official weather warnings in an average year of 2016 to 2025, plus 5 days for
  each hurricane it can expect in a year. Bands are fixed labels: Low below 1
  day a year, Moderate from 1, High from 2, Very high from 3.

  | Rank | Hub | Expected disruption days a year | Band | Main hazard |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Minneapolis | 2.90 | High | Winter |
  | 2 | Memphis | 2.44 | High | Winter |
  | 3 | Denver | 2.21 | High | Winter |
  | 4 | Kansas City | 2.20 | High | Winter |
  | 5 | Chicago | 2.03 | High | Winter |
  | 6 | Dallas | 2.02 | High | Winter |
  | 7 | Houston | 1.96 | Moderate | Hurricanes |
  | 8 | Miami | 1.85 | Moderate | Hurricanes |
  | 9 | Indianapolis | 1.56 | Moderate | Winter |
  | 10 | Atlanta | 1.24 | Moderate | Flooding |
  | 11 | Columbus | 0.99 | Low | Winter |
  | 12 | Phoenix | 0.42 | Low | Severe storms |

  **Read it as groups, not as an exact order.** The system rates this ranking
  low confidence, for two reasons:

  - For 10 of the 11 pairs of neighbouring hubs, the gap is too small to settle
  their order, given how much each hub's figure changes from year to year. Only
  Columbus and Phoenix are clearly apart.
  - First place depends on how much each hazard counts. With hurricanes counted
  double, or winter counted half, Miami comes first; with floods counted double,
  Memphis does. With winter counted double, the top three stay Minneapolis,
  Denver and Memphis.

  The band lines are round numbers, not natural breaks: Dallas is 0.02 days
  above the High line, and Houston, just below it, cannot be told apart from it.

  ## What the score counts

  - **Winter:** winter storm, blizzard, ice storm and lake-effect snow warnings.
  - **Flooding:** flash flood warnings.
  - **Severe storms:** tornado and severe thunderstorm warnings.
  - **Hurricanes:** the hurricanes and tropical storms a year the hub's county
  can expect, from the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA).

  Time is what counts: a day under any of these warnings is one disruption day,
  so a long warning weighs more than a short one. Each expected hurricane counts
  as 5 days, a figure checked against the warning records at Miami and Houston.

  ## What it cannot tell you

  - **Whether a hub closed.** A warning forecasts dangerous weather; it is not a
  closure record.
  - **What happens after a warning ends,** such as closed roads or power cuts.
  Winter's effect on roads lasts longest, so the score may understate winter.
  - **The future.** It describes 2016 to 2025 and is not a forecast.
  - **Flood risk at the exact site.** The score sees only each hub's county and
  an assumed location: its city's main airport (Love Field for Dallas).
  - **Heat, cold snaps, wildfire and earthquakes,** which it does not cover.

  ## What the investment decision still needs

  - How much each hub matters to the business.
  - What each upgrade would cost.
  - Flood risk at each hub's real address.
  - The company's own closure records, to show how long weather really shuts
  each hub.

  ## Data and period

  Weather warnings for 2016 to 2025 come from the National Weather Service,
  through Iowa State University's Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive. Hurricane
  figures come from FEMA's National Risk Index (December 2025 release, records
  from 1851 to 2024). Daily snowfall and rainfall for 2016 to 2025 come from
  NOAA airport weather stations, through ACIS, the data service of NOAA's
  Regional Climate Centers. They answer questions such as how many days had
  snow, and are not part of the score. All data was collected on 24 November
  2026.

  ## Asking the agent

  Once the system is installed, someone on the team starts the chat page with
  the command `uv run wra serve` and opens http://127.0.0.1:8000/ in a browser
  on the same computer. Questions are typed in plain English, for example "Which
  hubs in the Midwest are most exposed to winter disruption?" or "What if a
  hurricane counted double?". A what-if is labelled as such and leaves the
  official ranking unchanged.

  An AI model, Anthropic's Claude, writes only a short verdict. Each number in
  it is checked against the stored figures before the answer appears, and the
  tables beside it are built without the AI. Each answer with figures shows its
  sources, period and confidence. If an answer fails the checks twice, the page
  shows a plain error instead. Follow-up questions keep the context. Questions
  the data cannot answer, such as a city with no hub or next week's weather, are
  marked Out of scope. A typical answer has taken about five seconds, and each
  question is a paid request to Anthropic.

  Index dataset API or downloadable datasets but is not endorsed by FEMA. The
  Federal Government or FEMA cannot vouch for the data or analyses derived from
  these data after the data have been retrieved from the Agency's website(s).
