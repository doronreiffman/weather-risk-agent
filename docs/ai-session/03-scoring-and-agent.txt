  With ten noisy years, an average could easily have come out somewhat higher or
  lower. The test measures how much from the spread of the yearly figures. That
  gives 0.24 days for the pair. The gap between the averages is 0.27, a little
  bigger, so "Houston has more flood exposure than Miami" passes, just.

  Why hurricanes can't get the same test. FEMA gives one number per county and
  no yearly figures: Miami 0.305 storms a year, Houston 0.184. The gap is 0.12
  storms a year, which is 0.6 disruption days. Without yearly figures there is
  no spread to measure, so we can't tell from FEMA's number alone how solid that
  gap is. This decides the confidence label on the hurricane half of the
  assignment's Miami against Houston question.

  The three options:
  1. Cap at medium. We can't test the gap, so a hurricane answer is never
     labelled high. The reason line says FEMA gives one long-run figure with no
     yearly values.
  2. Leave the test out. The label rests only on whether FEMA has a figure for
     the county. It does for every hub, so hurricane answers come out high.
